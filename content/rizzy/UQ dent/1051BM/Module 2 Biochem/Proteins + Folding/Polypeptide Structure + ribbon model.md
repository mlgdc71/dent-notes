# Primary structure - Amino Acid
> amino acids are linked by ==peptide bonds== (covalent bond) in chains --> forming polypeptide chains then proteins

- this initial connection involves a ==condensation reaction== between the **amino group** of one amino acid and the **carboxyl group** of another  
- this forms the proteins primary structure

## beta-sheets
--> stabilized by ==hydrogen bonds== between ==adjacent segments== which hold sheets together in organized structure

>**Hydrogen bonds** are the main stabilizing force in **β sheets**, regardless of **==proximity==**.

### which hydrogen bond
MOSTLY INTER CHAIN
- these connect ==strands that can be far apart== in the amino acid sequence

SOMETIMES INTRA
- when sheet ==**conformation**==
## alpha helices
INTRA

- stabilised by ==intrachain hydrogen bonds==
	- these occur in the same polypeptide chain over a short distance

**UNLIKE Beta sheets**
--> alpha helices DO NOT have hydrogen bonds ==between strands== (interchain) across different segments
--> interchain h bonds are characteristic of beta sheets

>Think of **alpha helix** as a **spiral staircase** (local interactions within the chain) and a **beta sheet** as a **row of parallel or antiparallel paths** connected by hydrogen bonds across different strands.

alpha --> same polyp chain
beta --> different strands
