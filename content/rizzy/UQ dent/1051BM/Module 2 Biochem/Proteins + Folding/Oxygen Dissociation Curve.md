>**Myoglobin has a hyperbolic oxygen dissociation curve, releasing oxygen only at very low oxygen levels, whereas haemoglobin has a sigmoidal curve, allowing ==cooperative binding== and release of oxygen depending on oxygen levels.**

# Haemoglobin
>sigmoidal (curved) oxygen dissociation curve

allows for ==**efficient**== **load and unload** of oxygen
--> depending on oxygen availability

due to its primary function of a transporter --> which works to ==**pick up AND release**== oxygen
the sigmoid curve facilitates it
# Myoglobin
>==hyperbolic== oxygen dissociation curve

This means oxygen is released ==**only**== at **very low oxygen levels**

Myoglobin actually has a higher oxygen capacity relative to haemoglobin due to tighter binding at ==**lower concentrations**==

>think of myoglobin as a ==storage unit== that holds oxygen tightly until its **REALLY** needed

myo- means muscle
