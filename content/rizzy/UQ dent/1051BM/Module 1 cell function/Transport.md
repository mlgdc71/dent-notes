# NAK Pump (NKATPase)
- The **sodium-potassium pump** is an essential membrane transporter.
- It moves **3 Na+ ions out** of the cell and **2 K+ ions in** per ATP ==hydrolyzed.==
	- This generates a ==**differential concentration gradient**==
- This process **maintains the electrochemical gradient** across the cell membrane.
	- =="cellular electrochemical gradients."==
- The pump **is ==electrogenic**,== contributing to the cell's resting potential.
	- Generates voltage across a membrane

**Common mistake to avoid:** Confusing the ions and their directions. The correct current direction is ==**3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in**.==

>The main electrogenic pump of plants, fungi and bacteria is a proton pump

# Cotransport
>Cotransport is NOT a form of passive transport where the movement of one solute occurs independently of the movement of another solute.

- **Cotransport** involves moving ==two substances simultaneously==, with one driving the movement of the other.
	- Essentially, there is ==chemical energy== resulting from movement of molecules already passively diffusing
	- This **generated chemical energy** is used to transport another substance against the concentration gradient

![[Pasted image 20260302175025.png]]

- It is a type of **active transport**, requiring energy, typically because it moves substances against their concentration gradients.
	- **THE ENERGY IS NOT IN THE FORM OF ATP**

 Think of **"co"** as **"together"**—cotransport moves substances **together**, often using energy (active process).

# Brownian Motion of Passive Diffusion
#memorise 
- Passive transport is a **simple passive process** and only effective for small or nonpolar molecules.
- **Driven by ==random molecular motion==** (Brownian motion).

# Osmosis
Is movement of water across a semipermeable membrane
- It plays a vital role in **regulating water movement** between body compartments such as ==**extracellular fluid (ECF)** and **intracellular fluid (ICF)**.==
- Proper osmosis maintains **cell volume** and **fluid balance** in the body.
- The **movement of sugars** or other solutes is governed mainly by **diffusion** and **active transport**, NOT osmosis.

# Facilitated Diffusion
**Facilitated** diffusion is a type of passive transport that uses transport proteins to help ==hydrophilic== substances move along their concentration gradient without using energy.

- Common mistake: Confusing **facilitated diffusion** with **simple diffusion**; the key difference is the involvement of **transport proteins**.
- Involves transport proteins like channels or carriers

## Channel vs Carrier
When asked about **how molecules cross the membrane**, recall that **channel proteins create pores** whereas ==**carrier proteins change shape**==. [[Exo + Endocytosis|Receptor-mediated endocytosis]] is a special process involving vesicles, not simple diffusion.

- **Channels** are pore-forming proteins that allow specific ions or molecules to pass through **by diffusion**.
- **Carriers** ==bind specific molecules and **change shape**== to shuttle them across the membrane.
	- blob
- **Facilitated diffusion** depends on the structure and function of either channels or carriers.
