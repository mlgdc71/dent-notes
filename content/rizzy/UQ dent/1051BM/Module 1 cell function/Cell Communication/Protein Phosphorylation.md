>Protein phosphorylation is the process of adding phosphate groups to proteins, which is mediated by [[Enzymes|enzyme]] kinases.

- Phosphorylation can result in **activation** or **inactivation** of the target protein.

- **Protein phosphorylation** causes **conformational changes** in proteins.
- These changes can modify **protein-protein interactions** and affect **cellular location**.
- ==The main result of phosphorylation is the **activation or inactivation** of the protein.==
# Amino Acid Phosphorylation
- The amino acids **commonly phosphorylated** in proteins are **Tyrosine (Y), Threonine (T), and Serine (S).**
	- YTS
- **Phosphorylation** is a common **post-translational modification** that regulates protein activity.
- The amino acid **Lysine** is **not** typically phosphorylated; it is more often ==acetylated or methylated.==

# Cascades
>What is the primary role of phosphorylation cascades in cell communication?
>
>==They activate or inactivate target proteins, leading to cellular responses.==

- **Phosphorylation cascades** are processes where proteins are modified by ==adding phosphate groups.==

>Their primary function is to **activate or inactivate target proteins**


- These cascades **regulate cellular responses** such as ==growth, division, or apoptosis.==
- They are central to **cell signaling pathways**, especially in response to external signals.

Common mistake to avoid: Confusing the role of phosphorylation with structural or energy-related functions; phosphorylation is primarily about protein activity regulation, not providing energy or forming cell structure.
