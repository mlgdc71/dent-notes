- The **three stages of cell signalling** are **reception**, **transduction**, and **response**.
- During **reception**, signals are ==detected by cell receptors.==
- **Transduction** involves the ==relay of signals ==through a pathway, often involving second messengers.
- The **response** is the cellular action triggered by the signal.

[[Enzymes]] are usually important in this

# Secondary Messengers
- **Second messengers** are molecules that transmit signals from outside to inside the cells to trigger specific responses.

> Common second messengers include **cAMP, cGMP, IP3, DAG, Ca2+,** and **K+**.

- They help amplify and distribute signals within the cell, often generated or released upon receptor activation.

## cAMP
produced from [[Enzymes|adenylyl cyclase]] after getting activated by G proteins

- **cAMP** acts as a **second messenger** to propagate signals inside the cell.
- ==This process of conversion is crucial in many signaling pathways, including hormonal signaling.
	- The process is part of ==**intracellular communication==**, transmitting signals from the cell surface to interior.
