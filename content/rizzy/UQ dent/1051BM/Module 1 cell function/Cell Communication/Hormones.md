# Steroid

[[Receptors]]
-  **Steroid hormones** are lipophilic (fat-soluble) molecules.
- They **cross the cell membrane** easily due to their solubility.
- Inside the cell, they **bind to intracellular receptors** to influence gene expression.
- **G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs)** are not involved in steroid hormone signaling.
- A common mistake is to think hormones bind to G-protein coupled receptors or are only secreted into the bloodstream without entering cells.

# Hydrophilic Signalling Molecules
- Hydrophilic signalling molecules are water-soluble.
- They **typically bind to ==cell-surface (extracellular) receptors**==, which are located on the cell membrane.

# Hydrophobic Signalling Molecules
> these ones can pass through the [[Phospholipid Bilayer]]

- **Intracellular receptors** bind to **hydrophobic signalling molecules**.
- These receptors are located ==inside the cell==, typically in the cytoplasm or nucleus.
- Hydrophobic molecules can pass through the cell membrane easily due to their nonpolar nature.
- **Intracellular receptors** are typically used by **lipophilic (fat-soluble)** molecules, such as steroid hormones.
- **Common examples** include steroid hormones like **==cortisol and estrogen.==**
- Be cautious: many might assume all receptors are **membrane-bound**; remember that **intracellular receptors are different**.
