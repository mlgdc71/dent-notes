>G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) typically interact with heterotrimeric G proteins to control cellular activities by regulating enzymes, ion channels, and signal transduction pathways.

- GPCRs (G protein-coupled receptors) **interact with ==heterotrimeric ==G proteins**, not ==monomeric== G proteins.
- These G proteins **regulate key components** such as enzymes, ion channels, and signal transduction pathways.
- It is a common mistake to think they interact with monomeric G proteins; always remember **heterotrimeric G proteins** are involved.

>"G" in G-protein stands for **hetero-trimeric** when associated with GPCRs.

- **G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs)** are a large family of **membrane proteins**.
- They are characterized by having **seven transmembrane spanning domains** ==(**7 TMDs**).==
## GPCR Subunits
- G proteins in GPCR signalling are composed of **Alpha (α), Beta (β), and Gamma (γ)** subunits.

# G Protein
>Activated G proteins regulate effector molecules by either stimulating or inhibiting enzymes such as adenylyl cyclases and phospholipases, leading to the production of second messengers.

## GPCR Ligand Binding
- **GPCRs** involve **ligands** (binding molecule) binding to **membrane receptors**.
- Once activated, they stimulate **G proteins**.
- G proteins then activate downstream **effectors** like **channels** and **enzymes**.
- The **receptors** involved are located **on the membrane**, typically **at the cell surface**.

>**Ligand binds → receptor activates G protein → G protein activates effectors**

# Effectors
- **G proteins** activate or inhibit **effector molecules** such as **adenylyl cyclases** and **phospholipases**.
- This modulation leads to the **production of second messengers** which relay signals inside the cell.
- The process is a common pathway in **signal transduction**.
- **Common mistake:** Confusing activation with inhibition; remember that G proteins can do ==both depending on the effector.==
