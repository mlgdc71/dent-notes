- A **receptor** is a protein that **binds to a ==signaling molecule** (like a hormone or neurotransmitter==).
- Binding causes the receptor to ==**change shape**== and **trigger a cellular response**.
	- Cellular responses such as ==gene activation, enzyme stimulation, and rearrangement of the cytoskeleton.==
- Receptors are essential for **cell communication** and **response planning**.
> **Bind to a signaling molecule and triggers a response by ==changing shape**==.

# Steroid
- **Steroid receptors are located ==inside the cell==** (cytoplasm or nucleus) and **interact directly with [[Hormones|steroid hormones]]
- **They are not located on the cell membrane**; therefore, they do **NOT** mainly interact with ==peptide hormones or neurotransmitters.==
	- These receptors are **intracellular**, contrasting with membrane receptors for other hormone types.
- A common mistake is to think all receptors are on the cell surface or to confuse steroid receptors with peptide hormone receptors.

- Think of ==**steroids penetrating the cell membrane easily**== and then binding **inside the cell**.
- Remember: **Steroid hormones cross cell membranes**, so their receptors are **inside** the cell, not on the surface! 💡

# Ion Channel
- **Ion channel receptors** are proteins that open ion channels when activated, allowing specific ions to pass through the cell membrane.

## Ligand Gated Ion channels + nicotinic aceltyne receptor (nAChR)

>A nicotinic acetylcholine receptor is an example of an ion channel receptor; it is a ligand-gated ion channel that opens in response to the **binding of acetylcholine** (acetylcholine binding) to allow flow of Na+.

- The ==**nicotinic acetylcholine receptor** is a well-known example of a **ligand-gated ion channel**.==
- **Ligand-gated ion channels** are activated by binding of a chemical messenger (ligand), such as neurotransmitters.

>🧠 Remember: "Nicotinic" is linked to **nicotine**, which directly influences **ion channels** in nerve cells. Think of **Nicotinic = N for Nicotine and N for Nerve cell ion channel**! 🚀
