### 1. Basic Cellular Transport & Signalling Mechanisms

- **Definition:** **Cellular transport** is the movement of materials (like ions, nutrients, and waste) across the cell membrane. **Cellular signalling** is the process by which a cell detects, processes, and responds to chemical or physical signals from its environment or other cells.
    
- **Relevance to your course:** This is the foundation of cellular physiology. You will likely study passive vs. active transport, osmosis, and the sodium-potassium pump. For signalling, you will look at how molecules (ligands) bind to cell receptors, triggering a cascade of intracellular reactions (signal transduction). You will quickly see how these microscopic mechanisms are the exact processes that allow neurons to fire, muscles to contract, and hormones to cause widespread changes in the body.
    

### 2. Principles of Biochemistry

- **Definition:** The study of the chemical processes and substances that occur within living organisms.
    
- **Relevance to your course:** The course profile explicitly mentions preparing by reading [[The Chemistry of Life]] in the Campbell Biology textbook. You will need to understand the four major macromolecules: proteins (especially enzymes and membrane channels), lipids (cell membranes), carbohydrates (energy), and nucleic acids (DNA/RNA). You will also likely cover cellular respiration—how cells break down glucose to generate ATP (energy), which is the fuel required for every other key mechanism (like locomotion and active transport) in the course.
    

### 3. Neuronal Structure & Function

- **Definition:** The anatomy (structure) of nerve cells (neurons) and how they generate and transmit electrical and chemical signals (function).
    
- **Relevance to your course:** This topic bridges the gap between single-cell biology and whole-organism control. You will apply your knowledge of **cellular transport** to understand how ion channels create "Action Potentials" (electrical impulses). You will also apply your knowledge of **cellular signalling** to understand synapses—how one neuron releases neurotransmitters to communicate with another neuron or a muscle cell.
    

### 4. Motor Mechanisms & Locomotion

- **Definition:** The biological processes that enable movement. At a cellular level, this involves motor proteins moving along the cytoskeleton; at the organism level, it involves muscle contraction moving a skeleton (locomotion).
    
- **Relevance to your course:** You will likely learn the [[Sliding Filament Theory]] which explains how protein filaments (actin and myosin) slide past each other to contract a muscle. This topic perfectly integrates the previous modules: it requires a signal from a **neuron**, the release of calcium ions via **cellular transport**, and the use of ATP generated from **biochemistry** to produce physical movement.
    

### 5. Circulation & Gas Exchange

- **Definition:** **Circulation** is the mass transport of blood, nutrients, gases, and waste throughout the body via a pump (the heart) and vessels. **Gas exchange** is the physical process of absorbing oxygen from the environment and releasing carbon dioxide.
    
- **Relevance to your course:** This module scales physiology up to the organ-system level. You will learn why we breathe: because our cells need oxygen to perform **biochemical** cellular respiration to make ATP. You will study how simple cellular diffusion of gases across a membrane is integrated with complex whole-organism structures (like lungs, gills, and capillary beds) to ensure every cell in a massive organism stays alive.
    

### 6. Endocrine System

- **Definition:** The collection of glands that produce and secrete hormones directly into the circulatory system to regulate distant target organs, controlling metabolism, growth, and homeostasis.
    
- **Relevance to your course:** While the nervous system provides rapid, short-term communication, the endocrine system provides slow, long-lasting communication. This topic will bring you full circle back to **cellular signalling**. You will learn how hormones travel through the **circulation**, bind to specific cellular receptors, and alter the inner **biochemistry** of target cells to keep the entire organism balanced.

### Tips for Success in BIOM1051 Based on the Course Profile:

- **Review the Chemistry Early:** As the profile suggests, if it has been a while since you studied chemistry, read **Unit One: The Chemistry of Life** in Campbell Biology immediately. Physiology makes zero sense if you don't understand basic ions, gradients, and molecular bonds.
    
- **Look for the "Big Picture":** The profile states, "Of key importance is the integration of concepts." When studying for exams, do not just memorize how a single cell works. Ask yourself: How does this cellular process affect the whole tissue, the whole organ, and the whole human?
    
- **Prepare for the Labs:** You have four 3-hour practicals that use animal specimens. Because these are hands-on and occur irregularly throughout the semester, review the specific lab manual before you walk in so you can connect the wet-lab anatomy to the cellular physiology you are learning in lectures.
