# Notochord
- The **notochord** is a **==rod-like structure==** that forms during embryonic development which is made from **migrating ==ectodermal cells**.==
- The **main function** of the notochord is to ==**provide support** ==to the ==**primitive embryo**.==
- The notochord is a **crucial structure** in early vertebrate development, acting as a **scaffold** for the developing spinal cord.

- notochord is NOT formed from ectoderm but from migrating mesodermal cells.

# Somites
[[Embryology and development|Somitogenesis]]

- Somites are blocks of ==mesodermal tissue== in developing embryos.
- They give rise to structures such as **skeletal muscle**, **cartilage**, **tendons**, **endothelial cells**, and **dermis**.
- **False statement**: somites do _not_ give rise to all tissue types; specifically, they do _not_ form bones or certain other tissues directly.
- Common mistake: learners might think somites form **all tissues** in the body, but they primarily contribute to structures of the ==axial skeleton and associated tissues==.
	- **SCD** — **S**omite for Skeletal muscles, Cartilage, and Dermis

# Primitive Streak
>The midline structure where cells migrate during gastrulation, leading to the formation of mesoderm and notochord.

# Neural Crest Cells
>Cells that detach from the neural tube and contribute to most facial and head structures.
