In **DENT 1020**, the periodontium is one of the most critical concepts you will study because it represents the "support system" of the teeth. If the tooth is like a house, the periodontium is the foundation and the ground it sits on.

The periodontium consists of **four main components** that work together to keep your teeth anchored in your jaw:

### 1. Gingiva (The Gums)

- **What it is:** The visible soft tissue that covers the alveolar bone and surrounds the neck of the teeth.
- **Function:** It acts as a protective seal. In a healthy state, it is "stippled" (looks like orange peel) and pale pink.
    

### 2. Periodontal Ligament (PDL)

- **What it is:** A group of specialized connective tissue fibers that attach the tooth to the alveolar bone.

- **Function:** It acts as a **shock absorber**. When you bite down on something hard, the PDL allows the tooth to move slightly so it doesn't shatter. It also contains nerves that tell your brain how hard you are biting.
    

### 3. Cementum

- **What it is:** A thin, bone-like layer of hard tissue that covers the **root** of the tooth.
    
- **Function:** Its primary job is to provide a surface for the Periodontal Ligament (PDL) fibers to anchor onto the tooth.
    

### 4. Alveolar Bone

- **What it is:** The part of the jawbone (maxilla or mandible) that contains the tooth sockets.
    
- **Function:** It provides the rigid housing that supports the teeth. Interestingly, this bone is "dynamic"—if you lose a tooth, the alveolar bone will eventually resorb (melt away) because it no longer has a purpose.
    

---

### Why this is important for DENT 1020:

1. **Periodontal Disease:** You will learn that while Dental Caries (cavities) affect the tooth itself, Periodontitis affects these four tissues. If the periodontium is destroyed by bacteria, the tooth will become loose and fall out, even if the tooth itself is perfectly healthy.
    
2. **Normal vs. Abnormal:** In your "normal structure and function" modules, you will learn to identify a healthy periodontium (no bleeding, firm gums) vs. a diseased one (pockets, bone loss, inflammation).
    
3. **Clinical Practice:** When you begin clinical simulations, you will use a **Periodontal Probe** to measure the space between the gingiva and the tooth (the sulcus) to check the health of the periodontium.
    

**Summary Memory Tip:**  
Think of the acronym **G-C-A-P**:

- **G**ingiva
    
- **C**ementum
    
- **A**lveolar Bone
    
- **P**eriodontal Ligament
