### 1. Dental and Oral Diseases

- **Definition:** Pathological conditions affecting the hard and soft tissues of the mouth, including the teeth, gingiva (gums), supporting bone, and oral mucosa.
    
- **Course Context:** You will focus primarily on the two most common global diseases:
    
    - **Dental Caries:** The localized ==destruction of susceptible dental hard tissue==s by acidic by-products from bacterial ==fermentation of dietary carbohydrates==.
        
    - **Periodontal Diseases:** Inflammatory conditions (**Gingivitis and Periodontitis**) affecting the tissues that support the teeth, usually caused by a buildup of dental plaque (biofilm).
        
    - **Oral Cancer & Lesions:** Understanding the deviation from "normal" to identify suspicious tissues.
        

### 2. Normal Structure and Function of the Oral Cavity

- **Definition:** The study of the anatomy (structure) and physiology (function) of the mouth in a [[**"state of health"**]].
    
- **Course Context:** You must know "normal" before you can identify "disease." Key areas include:
    
    - ==**Hard Tissues:** Enamel, dentine, and cementum.==
        
    - **Soft Tissues:** The oral mucosa (lining of the mouth), the tongue, and the dental pulp.
        
    - **Periodontium:** The specialized tissues that surround and support the teeth (gingiva, periodontal ligament, and alveolar bone).
        
    - **Functions:** Mastication (chewing), phonation (speech), swallowing, and the protective role of saliva.
        

### 3. Development and Eruption of the Dentition

- **Definition:** The biological process of tooth formation (**odontogenesis**) and the chronological movement of teeth from their developmental sites within the jaws into the oral cavity.
    
- **Course Context:** You will learn:
    
    - **The Stages of Development:** Bud, Cap, and Bell stages.
        
    - **Primary vs. Permanent:** The transition from the "baby" teeth (deciduous) to the "adult" teeth.
        
    - **==Chronology:==** The typical ages at which specific teeth erupt, which is vital for assessing a child's development and planning orthodontic or restorative care.
        

### 4. Maintaining a Healthy Oral Cavity

- **Definition:** The practice of preventive dentistry to prevent the onset, or stop the progression, of oral diseases.
    
- **Course Context:** This involves **Biofilm Management**. You will study:
    
    - **Mechanical control:** Brushing and interdental cleaning.
        
    - **Chemical control:** The use of fluorides to remineralize enamel and antimicrobial agents.
        
    - **Dietary modification:** Reducing the frequency of sugar intake to alter the oral environment.
        

### 5. Evidence-Based Patient Care (EBPC)

- **Definition:** The integration of the **best available research evidence** with **clinical expertise** and **patient values/preferences**.
    
- **Course Context:** Dentistry is no longer based on "this is how we've always done it." You will learn how to:
    
    - Search for scientific literature.
        
    - Critically appraise clinical studies.
        
    - Apply scientific findings to a specific patient’s case in your Case-Based Learning (CBL) sessions.

### Further Information Relevant to DENT 1020

#### The FDI Definition of Oral Health

Since your ECP specifically links to the **FDI (World Dental Federation)** definition, it is important to know that they define oral health as:

> "Multi-faceted and includes the ability to speak, smile, smell, taste, touch, chew, swallow and convey a range of emotions through facial expressions with confidence and without pain, discomfort and disease of the craniofacial complex."

- **Key Takeaway:** Oral health is not just the absence of cavities; it is integral to general health and well-being.
    

#### Case-Based Learning (CBL)

DENT 1020 uses CBL, which means you won't just memorize facts in isolation. You will be given a "Patient Case" (e.g., a 45-year-old smoker with bleeding gums).

- **The Goal:** You must use your knowledge of anatomy, microbiology, and social sciences to figure out why the patient has this condition and how to treat it.
    

#### Integration of Sciences

This course is "interdisciplinary," meaning it bridges the gap between:

- **Histology:** Looking at tooth tissues under a microscope.
    
- **Microbiology:** Studying the bacteria (like S. mutans) that cause decay.
    
- **Biomaterials:** Understanding the properties of the materials used to fix teeth.
    
- **Professionalism:** Learning how to communicate with patients and work within a clinical team.
    

#### Clinical Competency

Even though you are in your first year, the "competency building activities" are designed to prepare you for the clinic. This includes learning how to use dental instruments, ergonomics (how to sit so you don't hurt your back), and infection control protocols.`
